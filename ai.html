Why does AI even work

We will learn why modern AI works not just how it works. Neural networks shouldn't work at all they are very non-linear yet somehow we can always find a gradient (a max/min of a learning function) and compute these efficiently in polynomial time. A summary:

  • Sparse Compositionality

Deep learning approximates functions that arise in nature where their structure is compositional, hierarchical, and computed in polynomial time which implies sparsity meaning you can reuse and combine these structures to form larger ones as only a small subset is needed for any given construction. The functions in your brain that control vision, language, and motor control are structured exactly this way. You don't see an array of pixels you see an object which contains parts which themselves contain textures and corners/edges. Every sentence you hear and speak is a composition with rules meaning a hierarchy. Macro behavior in physics comes from composing many local interactions. Composing as in function composition. One function is fed into another aka higher order function or w/e they call it.

  • Genericity

The natural world is full of generic functions where there is an ability to find a minimum using gradient descent. Physics enforces smoothness and stability. Biology filters out problem signals and prefers modularity/generics. Noise masks high-frequency irregularities and highlights lower-order structures and real world data is noisy. Deep networks in early training have a low degree structure that generates large and stable gradients. The conjecture of genericity being one of the principals why deep learning succeeds is because all the functions we care about are generic.

Almost all linear algebra learned in undergrad is not used as now you live in the world of factoring (compact SVD), matrices have to be randomized (jack polynomials), eigenvalues are approximated, and preconditioning is needed as gradient descent is too slow because it 'zig-zags'. Causality

Any future medical AI is going to need Causal AI models. If I do X what will happen to Y? To paraphrase Glenn Shafer the basic idea is to bring back the probability tree to represent a step-by-step evolution of an observer's knowledge. If that observer is nature, then the steps in the tree are causes, and the probabilities in the tree express nature's limited ability to predict the causes.
